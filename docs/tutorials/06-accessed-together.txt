If there is one golden rule to remember when moving from a relational database to MongoDB, it is this: data that is accessed together gets stored together.
In a traditional SQL database, we are trained to normalize everything—splitting a single business object across half a dozen tables and stitching it back together at runtime with expensive JOINs. Today, using Heavy Metal dot Studio (hvymetl.studio), we are going to look at how transforming two everyday e-commerce workflows—Customers and Orders—eliminates unnecessary joins, simplifies your application code, and accelerates read performance in MongoDB.
Let's start with our Customer entity. In a relational schema, customer profile details live in one table, while shipping and billing addresses live in a separate Customer_Addresses table linked by a foreign key.
Think about how your application actually uses this data. Almost every time a user logs in, edits their profile, or reaches checkout, your app needs both their core account details and their saved addresses in the exact same request. Because a customer typically has a small, bounded number of addresses—a one-to-few relationship—keeping them in a separate table adds read overhead for zero benefit.
Here in Heavy Metal dot Studio, we embed Customer Addresses directly inside the Customer document as an array. Now, a single read fetches the complete customer profile and every associated address in one round-trip to the database.
Next, let's look at an Order—where relational fragmentation really hurts performance at scale. In SQL, displaying a single order confirmation or customer support screen often requires a four-way JOIN across Orders, Order_Items, Payments, and Shipments.
In the real world, an order is a self-contained business event. Line items belong exclusively to that order. Payment authorizations and captures belong to that order. Shipment tracking and delivery updates belong to that order. Your application retrieves all of them together whenever someone views their order history or tracks a package.
By embedding Order Item, Payment, and Shipment directly inside the Order document in Heavy Metal dot Studio, we collapse four relational tables into one cohesive, rich JSON document. Beyond cutting query latency, embedding also gives you an accurate point-in-time snapshot—preserving the exact item prices, payment state, and shipping details as they happened at checkout, without complex historical audit tables.
Look at the before-and-after picture. Six rigid relational tables—and the multi-table joins required to query them—are now two intuitive, high-performance MongoDB collections: Customers and Orders.
When you model around how your application reads and writes data—storing together what you access together—your schema maps directly to the objects in your code.
